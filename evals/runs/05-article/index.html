The quiet case for slow mornings

What happened when I stopped checking my phone before breakfast for a month.

By Mara Ellison · October 12, 2026 · 6 min read

For years my mornings began the same way: an alarm, a thumb, a screen. Before my feet touched the floor I had already read three headlines, two messages and one argument I had no part in. The day arrived pre-shaped by other people.

The experiment was simple. For thirty days the phone stayed in the kitchen drawer until I had eaten breakfast, made coffee by hand, and written one page in a notebook. Nothing heroic, just a small fence around the first hour.

The first week felt like withdrawal. The third week felt like getting my own mind back.

By the end of the month I was not more productive in any way I could measure. But I was calmer, and the calm lasted longer into the day than I expected.
